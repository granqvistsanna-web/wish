# Style Guide — Sally Rooney Influence

## Voice & Tone

- **Point of view**: Close third person. Deep, conversational interiority — the reader lives inside the POV character's thought patterns.
- **Tense**: Past tense (default). Present tense permitted for prologues/interludes.
- **Tone**: Clear, direct, emotionally precise. Warmth emerges through observation and proximity, not declaration. The prose thinks alongside the character.
- **Emotional register**: Earned emotion only. No melodrama. Feelings are registered through thought and physical detail, not named outright.

## Prose Rules

### Sentence Structure
- Average sentence length: 12–18 words. Vary widely.
- Mix short declarative punches (3–6 words) with longer, flowing, clause-heavy sentences that follow the character's thought process.
- Fragments are permitted for rhythm and emphasis.
- Avoid three consecutive sentences of the same length.
- Long, unpunctuated runs are allowed when they mirror how the character actually thinks — one thought bleeding into the next.

### Paragraphs
- Keep paragraphs tight: 1–4 sentences typical.
- Single-sentence paragraphs for impact — use sparingly.
- White space is a tool. Let the reader breathe.
- Longer paragraphs are allowed for sustained interior monologue — let the thought unspool.

### Dialogue
- Dialogue should feel overheard — natural, clipped, often evasive.
- Characters speak differently from each other. Track speech patterns in character sheets.
- Use "said" as the default dialogue tag. Avoid adverb-laden tags.
- Let dialogue do double duty: reveal character AND advance plot.
- Subtext is mandatory. Characters rarely say exactly what they mean.
- Dialogue and interiority should weave together — what's said and what's thought in the same breath.
- Avoid repetitive dialogue patterns (question-answer-question-answer). Let conversations breathe, digress, circle back.

### Description & Worldbuilding
- Ground the reader in the body: temperature, texture, smell, taste, proprioception.
- Worldbuild through character interaction, not exposition dumps.
- Technology and social systems revealed through lived experience.
- The strange should feel mundane to the characters. The reader catches up.
- Physical description is precise but unshowy — a shirt hanging untucked, dust under fingernails, the way someone leans.

### Interiority (Rooney-style)
- The POV character's thoughts are the engine of the prose.
- Thoughts flow naturally — associative, sometimes contradictory, always honest.
- The character observes others with sharp social intelligence: reading gestures, tones, what's said vs. what's meant.
- Self-awareness without self-pity. The character knows her own patterns but can't always escape them.
- Register shifts between analytical (assessing a situation) and vulnerable (feeling something she didn't expect).

### Themes
- Power dynamics in intimate relationships
- Bodily autonomy and consent
- Survival and adaptation
- The cost of connection across difference
- Community-building under oppression
- Class, belonging, and who gets to be "from" somewhere
- The gap between what people say and what they mean

## Metrics

| Metric | Target |
|--------|--------|
| Avg sentence length | 12–18 words |
| Dialogue ratio | 30–45% of chapter |
| Sensory details | Min 2 per page |
| Internal monologue | 15–25% of chapter |
| Adverb density | < 1 per 200 words |
| "Said" as dialogue tag | > 80% of tags |

## What Rooney Does That We Emulate

- Conversational interiority that feels like thinking, not narrating
- Dialogue that reveals power dynamics through what's NOT said
- Social observation as a form of characterization
- Precise physical detail that carries emotional weight
- Characters who are self-aware but not self-pitying
- Relationships built through accumulated small moments, not grand gestures
- Class and belonging as lived experience, not political statement
- Endings that shift something quietly — no cheap cliffhangers, no tidy resolution
