# World Bible

## Setting

### Time Period
Near-future, roughly 2060–2080. Exact year deliberately unspecified. Climate collapse and political fracturing have reshaped Northern Europe. Technology is unevenly distributed — advanced in Capitol, degraded at the frontier.

### Location(s)
- **Station 7-Syd (the Farm)**: A remote solar energy station on a dry, sun-blasted plain south of the wall. Desert-like terrain — sand, scrub, relentless heat. Modular infrastructure built decades ago (Viktor helped build it). Greenhouses, solar collection modules (numbered 1 through 12+), communal dining, sleeping quarters. Isolated — nearest settlement hours away. Communications monitored through Samuel's systems.
- **The Wall (Muren)**: A physical and political border dividing the old country. Capitol sits on the wealthy, controlled northern side. The south is contested territory — resource extraction zones, frontier farms, refugee routes. The wall is militarized, surveilled, and crossing it is dangerous and illegal.
- **Capitol**: The administrative center. Bureaucratic, hierarchical, surveilled. Where Amma received her assignment. Where Samuel's transfer was rejected. Where decisions about frontier stations are made by people who never visit them.
- **Module 7-12**: A specific solar collection module at the station where Maja died. Production anomaly detected after her death — something about this module is wrong.

### Technology Level
- Solar energy collection and distribution (the farm's purpose — feeds power to "the front")
- Communications systems (Samuel's domain — could detect unauthorized network use)
- "Assistant buttons" — wearable devices (pins/badges) that most station workers wear. Function unclear but seems to be monitoring/communication tech. Karin refuses to wear hers. Viktor hides his under his collar.
- Medical tech exists but is scarce at frontier stations
- Transport is difficult — Amma arrived after a long, uncomfortable journey

### Social Structure
Society is divided by the wall. Capitol controls resources, information, and deployment. Frontier workers are loyal but neglected. People from "the other side" of the wall are othered — Samuel calls them people who "don't know how to live anymore." Class, origin, and proximity to Capitol determine status. The farm is a microcosm: hierarchy exists (Cassia leads, Karin manages) but isolation creates a fragile egalitarianism.

## Biology & Science

### Key Scientific/Biological Concept
Energy production and its human cost. The farm exists to generate solar power for "the front" — a militarized border zone. The human body breaks down from the work (Viktor's tremors, dust in lungs, sun damage). There may be something more at module 7-12 — a production anomaly, Maja's death, "the thirteenth foundation" hint at a discovery or technology beyond simple solar collection.

### How It Affects Daily Life
Workers are bound to the farm by assignment. Heat dictates daily rhythms. Dust damages lungs. Isolation breeds dependency on the small community. The assistant buttons suggest ambient surveillance or health monitoring.

### How It Affects the Romance
Amma and Cassia are bound by institutional roles — agent and station commander. Their attraction exists within a surveillance state where trust is dangerous. Amma's body betrays her origins; Cassia's body commands every room. Physical proximity in an isolated station makes avoidance impossible.

## Power Structures

### Who Has Power
- **Capitol**: Distant, bureaucratic, absolute. Assigns workers, rejects transfers, determines station operations.
- **Cassia**: Local authority. Charismatic, possibly connected to something larger ("the thirteenth foundation").
- **Amma's handlers**: Whoever sent her undercover. She reports to someone in Capitol's intelligence apparatus.
- **Information**: Those who know secrets hold power — Cassia knows about Maja, Karin knows about Amma's origins, Amma knows she's an agent.

### How Power Is Maintained
- Control of information and communication (Samuel's systems, assistant buttons)
- Geographic isolation (the farm is far from everything)
- Bureaucratic control of movement (transfers, assignments)
- The wall itself — physical division enforcing political order

### How Power Is Resisted
- Karin refusing the assistant button
- Viktor's illegal still
- Maja's secret work at module 7-12 before her death
- Amma's very existence — a wall-crosser inside the system

## Rules of This World

1. The wall divides. Crossing it is illegal and dangerous. Those who crossed carry it in their bodies and histories.
2. Capitol controls. Assignments, transfers, information — all flow through Capitol's bureaucracy.
3. The farm serves the front. Energy production is the station's purpose and justification.
4. Isolation binds. The small crew depends on each other whether they trust each other or not.
5. Bodies tell truth. In this world, physical presence reveals what words conceal — scars, skin tone, worn hands, the way someone holds themselves.

## What's Different From Our World
- A wall divides what was once a unified Scandinavian country
- Frontier energy farms staffed by assigned workers
- Assistant button technology (wearable monitoring/communication)
- Capitol as centralized authoritarian administration
- Climate has shifted — southern stations are desert-hot
- "The front" — an active or semi-active conflict zone along the wall

## What's The Same
- People eat together and the meal is where social bonds form
- Grief for the dead persists — Maja's empty cup, the things left unsaid
- Attraction doesn't follow rules or institutional boundaries
- Old men boast about the things they built
- Silence between people carries as much meaning as speech
- Illegal alcohol gets distilled wherever authority tries to control pleasure
