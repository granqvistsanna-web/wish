# World Bible

## Setting

### Time Period
Near-future, approximately 60–80 years from now. The exact year is never stated. Society has fractured along a massive physical barrier — the Wall — built during a resource war that never formally ended.

### Location(s)
**Primary**: Station 7-Syd (Station 7-South) — a remote solar energy farm in an arid, sun-blasted landscape. Sand, dust, relentless heat. The air smells of ozone and heated metal. Goats graze the perimeter. The station comprises living quarters, a communal kitchen, greenhouses, and a field of energy-harvesting modules stretching to the horizon.

**Secondary**: The Capitol — the governing city on the "right" side of the Wall. Bureaucratic, stratified, climate-controlled. Amma's employer.

**Referenced**: The North Coast (Samuel's origin — rain, cold, poverty). The other side of the Wall (Amma's birthplace — conditions unknown but implied to be desperate). Sector 14 (a combat zone near the Wall). The Front (the ongoing conflict zone where energy is directed).

### Technology Level
Mid-level. Solar energy harvesting through modular arrays (the "modules") that require hands-on maintenance. Communication systems exist but are monitored and controlled. "Assistant buttons" — small wearable devices worn on clothing — serve an unclear purpose (monitoring? communication? compliance tracking?). Medical technology is adequate but not advanced. No AI, no automation of manual labor at the station level.

### Social Structure
A stratified society divided by the Wall. Capitol citizens have documentation, employment, rights. People from the other side are undocumented, feared, dehumanized ("they don't know how to live anymore"). First-generation Capitol residents (like Samuel) occupy a precarious middle ground. The Energy Department controls strategic infrastructure. Station workers are low-status but essential.

## Biology & Science

### Key Scientific/Biological Concept
Energy harvesting and the bodily cost of proximity to it. The modules produce energy for the Front, but long-term exposure damages the body — Viktor's trembling hands, his deteriorating hearing, the dust in the lungs. The farm consumes the people who work it. Maja's death at a module may be connected to something the modules produce or contain beyond energy.

### How It Affects Daily Life
Workers age faster than they should. The heat, dust, and radiation from the modules erode the body. The assistant buttons may monitor exposure levels — or something else entirely. Workers accept the damage as the cost of employment and purpose.

### How It Affects the Romance
Vulnerability is physical. Amma's old shoulder wound, Samuel's scar, Viktor's trembling — the body keeps score. Intimacy means letting someone see what the work (and the war, and the lies) have done to you. Trust is built through witnessing damage, not through declarations.

## Power Structures

### Who Has Power
The Energy Department and Capitol bureaucracy. They control employment, documentation, and the narrative about the Wall and the people beyond it. Cassia holds local power at the station. Amma carries institutional power as an undercover inspector — but it's borrowed power that could be revoked.

### How Power Is Maintained
Documentation and classification. The Wall itself. Control of energy infrastructure. Information asymmetry — the workers don't know what the modules really do, or what happened to Maja, or what the "thirteenth foundation" means. The assistant buttons may be surveillance.

### How Power Is Resisted
Karin refuses to wear her assistant button. Li's silence is its own resistance — or protection. Cassia asks Amma to report to her first, bypassing official channels. Samuel's restless questions. Small acts of non-compliance, secrets kept, information hoarded.

## Rules of This World

1. The Wall is real, physical, and defines social identity. Which side you were born on determines everything.
2. Energy is the currency of power. The Front consumes it. The stations produce it. Workers are expendable.
3. Everyone carries documentation — or the absence of it. The assistant buttons mark compliance.
4. The modules are dangerous over long exposure. This is known but not discussed.
5. Information flows upward to the Capitol and is never returned. What workers know is what they see with their own eyes.

## What's Different From Our World
- A physical wall divides civilization, with an ongoing resource war (the Front) on the other side
- Energy harvesting stations dot the landscape, staffed by small crews in harsh conditions
- Wearable compliance/monitoring devices (assistant buttons) are normalized
- Social mobility is nearly nonexistent — your origin is your fate
- The state employs undercover inspectors to monitor its own infrastructure workers

## What's The Same
- People form families in isolation. They eat together, argue, protect each other.
- The body breaks down from labor. Workers take pride in what costs them.
- Bureaucracy is indifferent to individuals. Paperwork outlives people.
- Grief for the dead is carried in small rituals — an unused coffee cup, an empty chair.
- Loneliness drives people to trust those they shouldn't.
