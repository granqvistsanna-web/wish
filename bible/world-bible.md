# World Bible

## Setting

### Time Period
Unspecified future. At least two generations after "the Partition" — the construction of the Wall dividing Capitol from the outer territories. Technology is functional but aging. The war has been ongoing long enough that no one remembers what peace looked like.

### Location(s)
**Primary: Station 7-Syd (Station 7-South)**
An energy production farm in the southern territories, far from Capitol. Flat, sun-scorched land. Dust and heat dominate. The facility produces energy for the front through a series of numbered modules (1 through 13). Module 7-12 is the most productive — and the most mysterious. The farm has greenhouses, living quarters, a communal kitchen, and maintenance workshops. Viktor built much of it by hand decades ago.

**Secondary: Capitol**
The walled city-state. Center of political and military power. Where Amma was trained. Cleaner, colder, regimented. The Energy Department controls stations like 7-Syd remotely.

**Referenced: The Wall (Muren)**
The physical and ideological barrier dividing Capitol from the outer territories. Crossing it is illegal and dangerous. People from the other side are treated as lesser — "they don't know how to live anymore." Amma crossed as an infant with her mother.

**Referenced: The Front**
An ongoing military conflict somewhere beyond the farm. Energy from stations like 7-Syd powers the war effort. The front is distant but ever-present — it shapes rationing, politics, and who matters.

### Technology Level
- **Module-based energy production**: The farm runs numbered modules that generate energy through a process not fully explained. The technology is functional but aging — requires constant maintenance.
- **Assistant buttons**: Wearable devices (small pins/buttons) used for communication and possibly tracking. Most staff wear them. Karin refuses to wear hers. Li's status unknown.
- **Communication systems**: Samuel manages the farm's network. It connects to Capitol. It can detect unauthorized transmissions.
- **The Thirteenth Foundation**: Something Cassia knows about. Possibly a hidden module, a buried technology, or a classified project connected to module 7-12.

### Social Structure
- **Capitol** rules. Energy Department bureaucrats control the stations from a distance.
- **Station staff** live semi-autonomously under a station commander (Cassia). Small community, isolated, interdependent.
- **Wall-crossers** (people from the other side) are second-class. If discovered, they face deportation or worse. Amma hides her origins.
- **The front** consumes resources and people. Getting stationed at a farm is better than getting sent to fight.

## Biology & Science

### Key Scientific/Biological Concept
The modules produce energy through a process tied to the land itself — possibly geothermal, biological, or something stranger. Module 7-12 showed a production anomaly after Maja's death, not before. The implication: Maja found something in the module that changed its output. What she found — and whether it killed her — is the central mystery.

### How It Affects Daily Life
Dust in the lungs. Heat that breaks joints. Sun that eats skin. The body pays the price for energy production. Viktor's trembling hands, his failing hearing — the farm takes from its workers.

### How It Affects the Romance
Amma's body carries her history — the shoulder injury, the skin that marks her as an outsider, hands worn wrong for a desk technician. Cassia reads bodies instinctively. The romance builds through physical proximity in a harsh environment: shared labor, shared meals, the vulnerability of exhaustion.

## Power Structures

### Who Has Power
- **Capitol and the Energy Department**: Ultimate authority. They sent Amma.
- **Cassia**: Local authority. Respected, possibly feared. Knows things she shouldn't.
- **Information**: Whoever knows the truth about module 7-12 and Maja's death holds real power.

### How Power Is Maintained
- Distance (Capitol is far away, communication is controlled)
- Surveillance (assistant buttons, Samuel's communication network)
- Secrets (everyone lies about something)
- Dependency (the farm needs Capitol's resources; Capitol needs the farm's energy)

### How Power Is Resisted
- Karin refuses the assistant button
- Viktor distills illegal spirits
- Amma exists — a wall-crosser embedded in Capitol's own system
- Silence (Li says nothing; silence is its own rebellion)

## Rules of This World

1. The Wall is absolute. Crossing it is a crime. Being from the other side is a permanent mark.
2. Energy production serves the front. Everything else is secondary.
3. Assistant buttons are standard issue. Refusing one is noticed.
4. The stations are self-governing under Capitol oversight. Station commanders have wide latitude.
5. The dead stay dead. There is no formal investigation process for deaths at remote stations. An "accident" is accepted unless someone pushes back.

## What's Different From Our World
- A walled society divided by the Partition
- An ongoing war that consumes all surplus
- Module-based energy technology with unexplained properties
- A surveillance-communication system (assistant buttons) embedded in daily life
- Remote stations operating as semi-autonomous communities

## What's The Same
- People form bonds, loyalties, resentments in close quarters
- Power corrupts; secrets corrode trust
- The body remembers what the mind tries to forget
- Love grows in the strangest soil — between people who shouldn't trust each other
- Grief changes a community. Maja's absence shapes everything.
