# Chapter 2 — The Modules: Beat Sheet

## Opening Beat: First Morning
- Amma wakes in Maja's bed. Barely slept. The shoulder is stiff from the unfamiliar mattress.
- She didn't dream. She never dreams. (This is a lie she tells herself.)
- The farm's morning sounds: pipes groaning, distant voices, the greenhouse vents cycling open.
- Kitchen: Karin is already there. Coffee, bread, a tersely communicated schedule. "Modules today. Start with the south bank."
- Karin's instructions are practical, no warmth, but she's put out the good coffee. A small thing. Amma notices.

## Beat 2: Walking to the Modules
- Amma walks out through the fields toward the module bank. Alone.
- The landscape: flat, enormous, the sky a white dome of heat. Dust underfoot. The modules grow larger as she approaches — metal towers with access panels, coolant pipes, sensor arrays.
- Interior: Amma reviews her mission parameters. Inspect the modules, assess the production anomaly, confirm or deny the accident report. File. Leave. Simple.
- Physical: the heat is already building. Her short black hair is wrong for this — too short to tie back, long enough to stick to her neck. Her hands, calloused from the wrong kind of work, wrap around her toolkit.
- She passes modules 1 through 6. Routine inspections. The technology is aging but functional. She takes readings, makes notes. This part is real — she knows how to do this. The technician cover isn't entirely false.
- The landscape swallows sound. She is very alone.

## Beat 3: Tal
- A sound behind her. Footsteps on dry ground.
- Tal appears. Young, angular, moving with the tight energy of someone who hasn't learned to pace himself. He doesn't announce himself. He just appears, walking parallel to her path, twenty meters off.
- Amma stops. "Can I help you?"
- Tal: clipped, abrasive. "Cassia wants the modules checked in pairs. Safety protocol."
- Amma: "I wasn't told about a safety protocol."
- Tal: "You're new."
- He walks with her, slightly behind, slightly to the side. Like a shadow that doesn't match. He watches everything she does — which panels she opens, which readings she takes, which notes she writes.
- Amma is irritated. She's used to surveillance but not this amateur, obvious kind.
- They exchange minimal words. Tal volunteers nothing about himself. When Amma asks how long he's been at the station, he says: "Long enough."
- Amma: "That's what Viktor says too."
- Tal: "Viktor talks too much." No humor in it. Just flat dismissal.
- Interior: Amma catalogs him. Young. Abrasive. Maja's replacement in some capacity she doesn't yet understand. He watches her the way you watch someone you expect to steal something.

## Beat 4: Module 7-12
- They reach module 7-12. The tower is physically identical to the others but Amma feels the difference before she reads it.
- She opens the main access panel. Standard interface. She runs diagnostics.
- The numbers are wrong. Not dramatically — subtly. Energy output is higher than the module's rated capacity. The production curve shows a step-change: a sudden increase that began in the weeks after Maja's death.
- Tal watches her face. "What?"
- Amma: "Calibration drift. Normal for this age of equipment." She lies smoothly, automatically. Her heartbeat stays even.
- But inside: this is real. Capitol's data flagged the anomaly and the field readings confirm it. Module 7-12 is producing more energy than it should be able to. Not less. More.
- She takes readings she doesn't record in her official notes. She memorizes numbers.
- Tal doesn't believe her. She can see it in the way he stands — weight shifted forward, jaw tight. But he doesn't press.
- The heat at module 7-12 is different. Warmer than the others, as if the ground itself radiates. Amma notices. Files it.

## Beat 5: The Walk Back — Cassia
- Late afternoon. Amma is walking back alone. Tal peeled off an hour ago, heading toward the greenhouse without explanation.
- Cassia appears on the path. Not waiting, exactly — walking from the direction of the south modules with a tool bag over her shoulder. But the timing is precise enough to be deliberate.
- They walk together. The light is amber. Their shadows stretch long across the dust.
- Cassia asks about the modules. Conversational. Easy. "How did the south bank look?"
- Amma gives partial truths. "Aging but functional. Standard maintenance should keep them running." She doesn't mention 7-12's anomaly.
- Cassia: "And 7-12?"
- Amma: "I'll need to run more diagnostics." Also true. Also evasive.
- Cassia nods. Doesn't push. The silence between them is comfortable, and Amma doesn't know what to do with that. She's used to silence as weapon or shield. Not as something shared.
- Cassia talks about the farm. How the modules sound at night — a low hum you stop hearing after the first week. How the desert changes color at sunset. Small observations, offered without agenda.
- Amma notices: Cassia's hands. Brown, capable, the nails cut short. The way she shifts the tool bag from one shoulder to the other with practiced ease. The way she walks through this landscape like it grew around her.
- A moment: Cassia points out where the old well used to be, before Viktor built the reclamation system. "Maja used to sit there," she says. "After shifts. Said the ground was cooler." A small pause. "I still walk past it sometimes."
- Amma doesn't know what to say. She says nothing. It's the right thing.

## Beat 6: Tal's Reprimand
- They arrive back at the station. Tal is there, coming from the greenhouse.
- Cassia's tone shifts. Still warm but with authority underneath. "Tal, you were assigned to the water system today."
- Tal: "I was checking the modules."
- Cassia: "With Amma. Who didn't request company." A beat. "Your assignment is the water system. Please do it."
- Tal's jaw tightens. He walks away without responding.
- Amma and Cassia exchange a look. Cassia: "He's still finding his place."
- Interior: Amma wonders if Cassia sent Tal to follow her, then reprimanded him for appearances. Or if Tal acts on his own. Neither answer is comfortable.

## Closing Beat: The Numbers
- Night. Amma's room. The shoulder, the mattress, the dark.
- She sits at the small desk with her toolkit and her notes. She compares the field readings from module 7-12 to Capitol's archived data.
- The divergence is clear. Production increased sharply in the week after Maja's death. Not gradually — a step change. Like something was activated. Or released.
- Before Maja's death: normal output, consistent with module specifications.
- After Maja's death: output exceeds rated capacity by seventeen percent. And it's still climbing.
- Amma stares at the numbers. In her training, anomalies had explanations. Equipment failure, calibration error, environmental factors. This doesn't fit any of them.
- She thinks about Maja at the old well, sitting on cooler ground after shifts. She thinks about the warmth she felt at module 7-12's base. Warmer than the others.
- She closes her notes. She doesn't write any of this in her report.
- She lies down. The mattress dips where Maja's body made its impression. Amma fits herself into the shape of a dead woman and counts until she sleeps.
