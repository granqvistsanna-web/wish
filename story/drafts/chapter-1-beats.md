# Chapter 1 — Arrival

## Overview
- **Chapter number**: 1
- **Title**: Arrival
- **POV**: Amma (close third person, past tense)
- **Setting**: The road to Station 7-Syd → station exterior → communal dining room → Amma's quarters
- **Time**: Late afternoon through evening, first day at the station
- **Opening hook**: Amma walking the last stretch to the station in brutal heat, counting her steps — establishing her coping mechanism and the physical world simultaneously.

---

## Beat Sheet

### Beat 1 — The Approach
**Action**: Amma walks the final kilometer to Station 7-Syd. The transport dropped her where the road ended. Heat, dust, flat terrain. The farm emerges from the haze — low buildings, solar module arrays stretching to the horizon.
**Emotion**: Controlled focus. She is counting steps (PTSD/control mechanism). Beneath the control: dread. She doesn't want to be here.
**Sensory anchors**: Cracked earth under boots, heat pressing on shoulders (the bad shoulder aches), dust coating her throat, the metallic taste of recycled water from her canteen, sun like a physical weight.
**Dialogue**: None — internal monologue only. She rehearses her cover story.

### Beat 2 — Karin at the Gate
**Action**: Karin meets her at the station perimeter. No greeting, no warmth — just assessment. "Du är teknikern." (You're the technician.) Karin takes her bag without asking, leads her through the compound in clipped commands. Points out buildings: dining hall, sleeping quarters, greenhouse, maintenance shed. Doesn't point out module 7-12.
**Emotion**: Amma reads Karin — practical, territorial, not hostile but not welcoming. Notes Karin's missing assistant button (everyone else seems to wear one).
**Sensory anchors**: Karin's hes voice (hoarse, like a smoker), the weight of the bag being taken, shadows between buildings after the open glare of the plain.
**Dialogue notes**: Karin speaks in imperatives. "Kom." "Här." "Middagen om en timme." Amma responds minimally — matching Karin's register.

### Beat 3 — The Room
**Action**: Karin shows Amma to her quarters. Small, functional. A narrow bed, a shelf, a window facing the modules. The room is clean but carries traces of its previous occupant — scratch marks near the headboard, a faded rectangle on the shelf where something used to sit, the mattress shaped by another body.
**Emotion**: A cold recognition — this was Maja's room. Nobody said so. Nobody needed to.
**Sensory anchors**: The smell of disinfectant not quite covering something older, the scratch marks under Amma's fingers, the mattress dipping where Maja's weight had worn it, the window's angle catching late sun.
**Dialogue notes**: Karin: "Middagen om en timme." Turns to leave. Pauses at the door. "Koppen med den blå kanten — den använder vi inte." (The cup with the blue edge — we don't use it.) No explanation. Leaves.

### Beat 4 — Dinner: The Table
**Action**: Amma enters the communal dining room. A long table. She catalogues the crew: Viktor at the head (white mustache, trembling hands, talking before she sits down), the twins Elias and Nora (side by side, nearly identical, watching her), Samuel (across from her, dirty-blond hair too long, dark circles, the scar from neck to shoulder visible above his collar, a crooked smile). Li at the far end — dark hair in a tight knot, eating slowly, eyes on her plate. One empty seat.
**Emotion**: Amma in assessment mode. Every person is a variable. She deploys the safe version of herself — polite, professional, slightly bland. She is performing "new technician."
**Sensory anchors**: The scrape of chairs, stew that smells of root vegetables and something smoky, Viktor's voice carrying over everything, the clink of spoons, Samuel's restless leg tapping under the table.
**Dialogue notes**: Viktor immediately asks her age, grade, where she trained. Mishears her answer (comedy beat — "Two?" "Eight." "Seven." "No, eight." "Two?"). Samuel watches the exchange, amused. The twins eat in silence.

### Beat 5 — Samuel Probes
**Action**: Samuel engages Amma in conversation. Chatty, associative, self-deprecating on the surface — but his questions are precise. Where did she train? How long in Capitol? What department? He tells anecdotes — his own arrival six years ago, the rain on the North Coast, how he ended up here. Weaves in observations: "You don't hold your spoon like someone from Capitol." Amma deploys safe truths: she loves the smell of mint, she prefers mornings.
**Emotion**: A duel disguised as dinner conversation. Samuel is testing her. Amma is parrying. Both know it. Neither says so.
**Sensory anchors**: Samuel's crooked smile that doesn't reach his eyes, his fingers turning his water glass, the scar moving when he swallows.
**Dialogue notes**: Samuel's speech is associative, jumping between subjects. He breaks off mid-sentence sometimes. Key line: something about the last technician — "Maja wasn't much for conversation either. Toward the end." Said lightly. Not light.

### Beat 6 — Cassia Enters
**Action**: The door opens. Cassia walks in. The room changes — Viktor sits straighter, Karin looks up from her plate, Li lifts her eyes for the first time. Cassia: long graying curly hair in a loose knot, clothes that fit like they were made for her, a face that is hard to stop looking at — not beautiful in a conventional way but completely present. She greets the room, then turns to Amma. "Du måste vara vår nya tekniker." (You must be our new technician.) She sits beside Amma. Close. Natural.
**Emotion**: Amma registers Cassia's physical presence before processing her words. Something in her chest contracts. This is unwelcome. This is dangerous. She catalogs the reaction and files it away.
**Sensory anchors**: The scent of something clean — not soap, something else — when Cassia sits down. The warmth of proximity. Cassia's hands on the table, unhurried. The way her voice is warm but her eyes take in everything.
**Dialogue notes**: Cassia asks about Amma's journey — genuine interest or performance? Amma can't tell. Cassia's speech: warm, direct, questions that make people open up. She calls Amma by her first name immediately, as if they're old friends.

### Beat 7 — Viktor's Story and Li's Silence
**Action**: Viktor launches into the story of building the farm. "These hands." The station produced thirteen percent of the front's energy last quarter — or was it eight? His trembling hands gesture broadly. Meanwhile, Amma watches Li. Li has not spoken. Li has not looked up since Cassia sat down. Li eats with mechanical precision. Nobody acknowledges this.
**Emotion**: Amma is cataloguing: Viktor (loud, proud, possibly declining), Li (a void in the room's social fabric — what is she?). The station's dynamics are becoming visible: Cassia as center of gravity, Karin as enforcer, Samuel as provocateur, Viktor as storyteller, the twins as silent witnesses, Li as the question no one asks.
**Sensory anchors**: Viktor's mustache moving when he chews, his hearing aid (or assistant button?) tucked under his collar, Li's chopsticks (or fork) moving at the same interval, Karin eating with efficient disinterest.
**Dialogue notes**: Viktor: boastful, interrupted by his own tangents. Amma gives him what he wants — impressed nods, brief questions. Samuel catches her eye across the table. Knows what she's doing.

### Beat 8 — After Dinner: Cassia Walks Her Back
**Action**: Dinner ends. People disperse. Cassia offers to walk Amma to her quarters — "so you don't get lost," said with a half-smile that suggests she knows the station is too small to get lost in. They walk in the cooling air. Cassia talks about the station: the routines, the work, the heat. Then, more carefully: "The last technician was very capable. Maja. She checked everything twice. Three times." A pause. "She was working on module 7-12 when she died."
**Emotion**: Amma's agent-brain activates. This is what she's here for. But Cassia's voice — the grief in it is real. Or performed. Amma can't tell and that's what makes Cassia dangerous.
**Sensory anchors**: Cooling desert air, the crunch of sand under their feet, the hum of the modules in the dark, stars visible in a way they never are in Capitol, Cassia's profile in half-light.
**Dialogue notes**: Cassia: "If you find anything unusual at the modules — anything at all — I'd like you to tell me first." Said directly. No hedging. Amma: "Before the official report?" Cassia: "Before anyone." A beat. "Can you do that?" Amma agrees. She doesn't know why.

### Beat 9 — Alone
**Action**: Amma in her room. Door closed. She sits on the bed — Maja's bed — and the mattress gives in the wrong places. She runs her fingers along the scratch marks near the headboard. Counts them. Twelve marks, evenly spaced. Then a gap. Then one more. Thirteen. She doesn't know what it means yet. She lies down. The shoulder aches against the thin mattress. She counts her breaths, the way she always does. Rehearses the day: every face, every word, every thing she noticed. Files it. Tries to sleep. Can't. Counts the scratch marks again in the dark.
**Emotion**: The mask comes off. Alone, Amma is tired, afraid, and aware that she has already been seen more clearly than she wanted — by Samuel, by Karin, by Cassia. The scratch marks are a dead woman's record of something. Amma is sleeping in her bed.
**Sensory anchors**: The mattress dip, the scratch marks' texture under fingertips, the hum of modules through the wall, her shoulder's deep ache, the dark pressing in, her own breathing measured and deliberate.
**Dialogue**: None. Internal monologue. Counting.

## Romance Progression
- **Stage**: First encounter. Attraction registered, immediately suppressed.
- **Key moment**: Cassia sitting close at dinner. The scent. Amma noticing Cassia's body before her words.
- **Subtext**: Cassia's request ("tell me first") creates an intimate conspiracy between them before any intimacy exists.

## Thematic Thread
- **Power and trust**: Everyone at this table has secrets. Amma's are institutional. What are the others'?
- **The body as truth**: Amma's body (skin, hands, shoulder) reveals what her words hide. Cassia's body commands space. The dead woman's body-shaped mattress.
- **Counting as control**: Amma counts because the world is ungovernable. Steps, breaths, scratch marks.

## Chapter-Ending Image
Amma in the dark, fingers on Maja's scratch marks. Twelve. A gap. Thirteen. She doesn't understand yet. But her body knows — the counting has already begun.
