# Synopsis

## Premise

Amma Lindqvist is an undercover technical inspector sent to a remote energy farm near the wall — the barrier that divides what remains of civilization from the irradiated territories beyond. Her mission: assess whether the farm's failing energy modules are sabotage or neglect, and report back to the Department of Energy. If she does her job well, she earns a transfer far from the wall and the warfare that killed her mother. But the farm's previous technician, Maja, died under circumstances no one will explain honestly. The deeper Amma digs into module 7-12's failures, the closer she comes to a secret the farm's commander may be willing to kill to protect — and the harder it becomes to betray the people she's been sent to surveil.

## Amma's Goal

**Primary**: Complete her inspection, file her report, get reassigned to a safe post in the interior. Survive. Disappear.

**Hidden**: Understand what happened to Maja — not out of justice, but because Maja's death pattern mirrors her mother's. An "accident" that was officially nothing. A woman who got too close to something.

## Amma's Obstacles

1. **Her cover is thin.** She doesn't look like Capitol-born. Her shoulder injury limits her physical work. Samuel already suspects her. One wrong detail and she's exposed — and exposed means dead, or worse, returned across the wall.

2. **Module 7-12 is a trap.** The production anomalies started *after* Maja's death. Something in that module is being hidden, possibly by the same people who welcomed Amma to dinner. Investigating it means risking Maja's fate.

3. **Cassia.** The station commander is warm, perceptive, and knows more than anyone. Amma is drawn to her — to her directness, her attention, the way she treats Amma as an equal. But Cassia may be connected to "the thirteenth foundation," and her warmth may be strategy. Trusting her could be fatal. Not trusting her may be worse.

4. **Loyalty is a weapon here.** Everyone on the farm is bound by shared isolation and mutual dependence. Amma's mission requires her to betray them. But these are people fighting to maintain something — energy, community, survival — under conditions the interior government ignores. Her mother died for the same kind of fight. Completing her mission means destroying what her mother believed in.

5. **Tal is watching.** The newcomer follows her, monitors her movements. He replaced Maja. Whether he's protecting the farm's secret or running his own investigation, he's a threat Amma can't gauge.

6. **Her own body.** The shoulder that never healed. The PTSD triggered by the farm's industrial sounds. Her body betrays what her words conceal — she is not who she claims to be, and every physical reaction risks exposure.

## The Hook

Amma arrives at the farm expecting a routine inspection at a failing energy station. At her first dinner with the crew, she sits in a dead woman's chair, drinks from a cup no one else will touch, and realizes every person at the table is performing for her benefit. She is not the only one here with a cover story.

## Act One — Setup (Chapters 1–5)

Amma arrives and establishes her cover as a Department of Energy maintenance technician. She meets the crew: Cassia's disarming warmth, Karin's blunt suspicion, Viktor's proud storytelling, Samuel's nervous chatter, Li's impenetrable silence, and the twins' quiet curiosity. She begins assessing the modules, but module 7-12 is off-limits — mechanical failure, she's told. Tal appears in chapter 2, following her into the fields. Samuel confronts her about inconsistencies in her story. Karin, unexpectedly, warns her to keep her origins quiet — revealing she knows Amma crossed the wall. By the end of Act One, Amma has found Maja's maintenance logs hidden in a secondary terminal. The logs stop abruptly three weeks before her death. The last entry: *"Output doesn't match input. Something is being fed."*

## Act Two — Confrontation (Chapters 6–15)

Amma secretly investigates module 7-12 while maintaining her cover inspection of the other modules. She discovers the module is producing more energy than its fuel input can account for — an impossibility unless there's an unregistered power source. The farm isn't failing. It's hiding surplus.

Her relationship with Cassia deepens. Late-night conversations in the greenhouse. Cassia speaks about the wall, about what the interior government refuses to acknowledge — that people on the other side are surviving, adapting, building. Cassia's vision: the farm as a node in a secret network funneling energy across the wall. The thirteenth foundation. Amma realizes Cassia isn't hiding incompetence. She's hiding treason.

Samuel's suspicion turns to something more complicated — he confronts Amma with what he knows, but instead of exposing her, he asks what she's really looking for. He arrived with Maja. He watched her become obsessed with module 7-12. He watched her stop sleeping, stop speaking to him. He doesn't know what she found, but he knows it consumed her.

Tal reveals his connection: his father worked on the original thirteenth foundation project and died for it. Tal is here to finish what his father started — or to find out who killed him. He and Amma are mirrors: both undercover, both grieving dead parents, both dangerous to each other.

### Midpoint

Amma accesses module 7-12 alone at night and finds the hidden power source — a biological energy system, something organic integrated into the module's infrastructure. Living tissue that converts radiation into usable power. Not engineered by Capitol. Brought from the other side of the wall. Maja didn't die because she found the secret. She died because she tried to report it, the same mission Amma is on now.

Amma must choose: file her report and condemn the farm, or protect the secret and become complicit in Cassia's treason.

## Act Three — Resolution (Chapters 16–20)

Amma's handler contacts her demanding the overdue report. She stalls. Cassia, sensing the shift, confronts Amma directly — not with anger, but with the truth. The biological system came from Amma's side of the wall. Her mother's people developed it. Cassia has been waiting for someone like Amma — someone who carries both worlds — to decide the module's fate.

The farm fractures. Viktor, who built the station with his hands, refuses to accept that it was built on a lie. Karin forces a reckoning: the crew must decide together whether to go public or go deeper underground. Li finally speaks — she's the biological system's caretaker. She's been silent because her work requires a kind of communion with the living module that language disrupts.

Tal makes his move. He's decided the thirteenth foundation must be exposed — not to Capitol, but to the people on both sides of the wall. He transmits a signal. Capitol responds. An enforcement team is dispatched.

The final chapters are a siege — not military, but bureaucratic and psychological. Amma must use her inspector credentials to delay the enforcement team while Cassia's network evacuates the biological components to other stations. Samuel runs interference on communications. Viktor refuses to leave the farm he built. The twins make a choice that costs one of them.

## The Ending

Amma files her report. It says the farm's modules failed due to age and dust erosion. Routine. She recommends decommissioning module 7-12 and reassigning staff. The report is a lie — her final, most consequential lie, and the first one she's chosen freely.

She does not get her safe interior posting. She asks to be reassigned to the next farm in Cassia's network. She walks toward the wall, not away from it. Her shoulder aches. She counts her steps out of habit, but she's stopped counting the days.

Cassia is already there when she arrives.

## Thematic Statement

Freedom is not escape from the wall but the choice of which side to stand on — and who to stand beside.
