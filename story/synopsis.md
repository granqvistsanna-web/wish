# Synopsis

## Premise
Amma Lindqvist, an undercover inspector for the Capitol's Energy Department, arrives at Station 7-South — a remote solar farm — to investigate a production anomaly in Module 7-12, the same module where a technician named Maja died weeks earlier. Posing as a replacement technician, Amma must navigate a tight-knit crew who are suspicious of outsiders, uncover what Maja discovered before she died, and resist the pull of connection in a place where everyone has secrets. Born on the wrong side of the Wall and carrying false papers, Amma's greatest danger isn't what killed Maja — it's that the people she's been sent to investigate are becoming the closest thing to family she's had since her mother died.

## The Hook
A woman with a fake name arrives at a farm where the last person to hold her position died under suspicious circumstances. Everyone watches her. She watches everyone back. But her shoulder aches, and the station forewoman treats her like an old friend, and the man who runs the communications system sees through her lies on the first day.

## Act One — Setup (Chapters 1–4)
Amma arrives at Station 7-South and meets the crew: Cassia, the charismatic forewoman who knows more than she reveals; Karin, the terse caretaker who immediately clocks Amma's origins; Samuel, the chatty communications officer who can't sit still and can't stop observing; Viktor, the aging builder whose body is failing; the silent Li; the twins Elias and Nora; and Tal, Maja's young, hostile replacement. Amma establishes her cover, begins her inspection of the modules, and encounters the first signs that Maja's death was not an accident. The production anomaly in Module 7-12 doesn't match any standard malfunction. Cassia asks Amma to report anything unusual to her first — before the Capitol. Samuel confronts Amma about her lies. Karin warns her to hide her origins.

## Act Two — Confrontation (Chapters 5–14)
Amma's investigation deepens as she discovers that Module 7-12 has been modified — someone altered its configuration to do something beyond energy harvesting. Maja figured it out and was killed for it, or killed by it. The "thirteenth foundation" — a hidden layer in the station's original architecture — may be the key. As Amma gets closer to the truth, she gets closer to the people: Samuel's vulnerability behind his chatter, Cassia's calculated warmth that might be genuine, Karin's fierce protectiveness. The romance develops through shared labor, proximity, and the slow erosion of Amma's defenses. Tal's hostility escalates — is he protecting something, or grieving? Li breaks her silence at a critical moment.

### Midpoint
Amma discovers what the thirteenth foundation actually is: the station was originally built for something other than energy production, and the modules can be reconfigured for a purpose that would change the war — or end it. Maja found this and tried to activate it. Now Amma must decide: report to the Capitol as ordered, or protect the people who are starting to trust her.

## Act Three — Resolution (Chapters 15–20)
Amma's dual identity collapses. The Capitol sends a follow-up team. Amma must choose between her mission and the station. The crew fractures — those who knew about the thirteenth foundation and those who didn't. The romance reaches its crisis: can you love someone who was sent to betray you? The final confrontation forces Amma to use everything she's learned — her technical skills, her relationships, her knowledge of both sides of the Wall — to protect the station and its secret. The cost is her ability to ever go back.

## The Ending
Amma destroys her own cover to save the station. She can never return to the Capitol. She has no documentation, no name, no position. But she has a place — the farm, the people, the work. The romance doesn't resolve neatly. Trust, once broken, rebuilds slowly. The last image is Amma's hands in the dirt of the greenhouse, her shoulder aching, the sun on her neck, and someone's footsteps approaching behind her.

## Thematic Statement
The only way to belong anywhere is to stop pretending you belong somewhere else.
